Instructor Guide (quick)

What students actually do.

  • Pick a U.S. firm/product and set weights that reflect a strategy (Balanced / Cost-driven / Risk-averse / Growth-seeker—or custom).

  • Use the simulator to produce a ranked list and a radar profile for the top pick.

  • Write a concise analysis that ties culture (fit vs. distance), system quality (stability, rule of law, corruption control), and economic development (GDP per capita, growth, market size) to cost, risk, and demand—then recommend entry mode & city.

How the game scores (under the hood).

  • Culture Fit = 100 − culture distance

  • Institutions = avg(political stability, rule of law, corruption control)

  • Market Potential = avg(market size, growth×10)

  • Cost Advantage = 100 − labor cost index

  • Logistics and Trade openness are direct scores (higher = better)

  • Final score = weighted sum of the six components (weights chosen by student).

Assessment shortcuts.

  • Look for alignment between stated strategy and slider weights.

  • Check that the #1 vs #2/#3 comparison explicitly uses national differences → managerial implications (e.g., “higher rule of law reduces opportunism → contractable entry mode feasible”; “greater culture distance → need for adaptation/support partners”).

  • Entry mode should logically reflect local institutions/culture/costs.

Common pitfalls to deduct for.

  • “Big market = better” without cost/risk discussion.

  • Culture distance mentioned but not tied to adaptation cost/HR/marketing.

  • Institutions named, but no link to contracting, IP, or corruption risk.

  • Entry mode chosen without explaining why it fits the country’s system.
